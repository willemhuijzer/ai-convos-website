# AI Convos project guidance

## Purpose

AI Convos is an Amsterdam meetup series for people who work deeply with AI. The talks create shared context; the conversation in the room is the main event. Keep the project grounded in specific experiences, candid lessons, and useful questions rather than hype or polished sales pitches.

## Where context lives

- Read [CONTEXT.md](./CONTEXT.md) for the project language.
- Read [EVENTS.md](./EVENTS.md) before writing or changing event content.
- Treat `EVENTS.md` as the factual event ledger. Treat `index.html` as a presentation of those facts, not an independent source of truth.
- Put durable working principles here and edition-specific facts in `EVENTS.md`. Do not make `AGENTS.md` a duplicate event database.

## Working with event information

- Model each gathering as an **edition** in one chronological series, even when its public name changed between AI Sessions, AI Connect, and AI Convos.
- Separate facts from format. Dates, venues, speakers, links, and prices belong to an edition; principles such as short talks, discussion, and time to connect belong to the series.
- Mark information as confirmed, provisional, unknown, or conflicting. `TBA` means unknown; never fill it from a nearby edition or a recurring pattern.
- Preserve disagreements between sources and surface them for resolution. Do not silently choose a date, venue, speaker, duration, or spelling when records conflict.
- Prefer direct organizer notes and final invitations over old website copy. Use the current site only as supporting evidence when the event ledger is incomplete.
- Use absolute dates including the year. Keep an event date distinct from the date on which it was announced.
- Distinguish the host organization from the physical venue. Credit both when known.
- Treat payment links and schedules as time-sensitive. Do not reuse them for another edition, and verify them before publishing.

## Format and editorial principles

- The format is intentionally evolving. Describe what happened at a past edition; do not retroactively force every edition into the current format.
- Short, diverse talks work because they give the room several starting points. Leave meaningful time before, between, and after them for questions and conversation.
- The questionnaire made the audience visible: who was in the room, how they used AI, and how they viewed its development. It was insightful but expensive to prepare. Preserve that learning when considering future audience-participation formats.
- Prefer concise, concrete language. Name the idea, experiment, failure, or lesson a speaker will share and why it creates a worthwhile conversation.
- Avoid generic claims such as “cutting-edge,” “revolutionary,” or “the future of AI.” The tone should be curious, informed, informal, and human.
- Do not overstate certainty or invent missing history. A short explicit unknown is better than plausible-looking copy.

## Updating the site

- Reconcile affected entries in `EVENTS.md` before changing event details in `index.html`.
- Keep editions in chronological order and preserve their historical public names.
- Check weekday/date combinations, organization and speaker spellings, address, start time, talk duration, and link freshness.
- When new evidence resolves a conflict, update the ledger and remove the obsolete conflict note in the same change.
